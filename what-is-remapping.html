The complete explanation

What is a remap, really?

No jargon, no sales pitch. If you've been quoted for one and want to understand what you're actually buying, this is the page.

Start here: your engine is a computer decision, thousands of times a second

Every engine built in the last twenty-odd years has a control unit — the ECU. It reads what the pedal is doing, how fast the engine is turning, how much air is coming in and how hot everything is, then decides how much fuel to inject, exactly when to ignite it, and how hard the turbo should blow.

Those decisions aren't calculated from scratch each time. They're looked up from tables of pre-set values written at the factory. A remap means rewriting those tables.

The six questions everybody actually asks

If there's spare performance, why didn't the manufacturer use it?

Because they write one file for the whole world. It has to survive poor fuel in one country and forty-degree heat in another, cope with owners who never service the car, and hit an emissions and tax bracket that suits the sales brochure. They also want the cheaper model in the range to stay slower than the expensive one — which is why two cars with the same engine often leave the factory with different power figures. Building for one vehicle, in one climate, driven by one person, leaves genuine headroom.

Does anything physically change on the car?

No. On a standard remap nothing is removed, cut, drilled or replaced. We connect to the diagnostic socket — the same one a garage plugs into — read the software off, rewrite it, and write it back. The car looks exactly the same when you collect it.

Is it safe for the engine?

It depends entirely on who writes the file. The manufacturer builds in margins: how hot things are allowed to get, how much pressure a cylinder can take, how lean the mixture is permitted to run. A good file moves fuel, air and timing together and leaves those margins alone. A bad file buys a bigger headline number by spending them — and it will still drive fine for a while, which is what makes it dangerous. That's the whole difference, and it's invisible from the driver's seat.

Can it be undone?

Yes, and this matters more than people realise. Before anything is changed we read your original factory file off and archive it against your registration. It can be put back at any point — before a dealer visit, before selling the car, or simply because you changed your mind.

What about insurance and warranty?

Insurance: declare it. It's a modification and an undeclared one can invalidate a claim, so we give you the specifics in writing to forward straight to your insurer. Most policies take it without drama. Warranty: if the vehicle is still under manufacturer cover, assume a remap affects it — we'll say so plainly before you book, and the archived original means it can go back to standard first.

Remap or tuning box?

A tuning box sits between the ECU and the engine and fakes the signals going past — the ECU thinks one thing is happening while something else actually is. A remap changes the decisions at source, which is more precise and lets us respect the safety limits rather than work around them. Boxes have one advantage: they unplug. If that matters more to you than quality of result, we'll tell you honestly.

How to judge a tuner

What separates a good file from a bad one

Anyone can buy the tool that loads a file. It takes twenty minutes and the car can't tell the difference. These are the questions worth asking whoever you use — including us.

"Is the file written for my car, or bought off a shelf?"

Same badge, very different story underneath. Mileage, service history, turbo condition and previous work all change what's safe to ask for.

"Will you log it before and after?"

Requested against actual boost, fuel trims, timing pulled, exhaust temperature. Without measurement, nobody knows what the file is really doing.

"Do you keep my original?"

If the answer is anything other than an immediate yes, walk away.

"What won't you do?"

A tuner who'll do anything you ask, including the illegal things, is telling you exactly how much care goes into the rest of it.
