Our story

My grandfather never touched a laptop in his life

He'd have had no interest in one. He also had a rule about explaining your own work that this business still runs on, decades later, on equipment he wouldn't recognise.

A workshop, a grandfather, and a boy who wouldn't stay out of the way

Long before any of this involved a laptop, there was an engine on a bench, a grandfather working on it, and a grandson who was supposed to be keeping out from under his feet and never once did.

You learn things in a workshop that you can't learn from a manual. That a noise tells you more than a fault code. That the customer standing in the doorway deserves a straight answer even when it isn't the one they want. That the cheap fix always comes back, usually at the worst possible moment and usually costing more than doing it properly would have in the first place.

None of that has anything to do with software. All of it decides how a remap gets written.

"If you can't explain what you changed and why you changed it, you had no business touching it."

— The rule this workshop still runs on

Then and now

From spanners to software

Engines stopped being purely mechanical. The adjustments that used to be made with a screwdriver moved into tables inside a control unit. The thinking behind them didn't change at all.

You still have to know what the engine is doing, why it's doing it, and where the limit sits before you go anywhere near it. The screwdriver became a laptop. The judgement is identical, and it's still the part that takes years rather than weeks to build.

That's the whole business, really. Same trade, different instrument — now applied to cars, vans and farm machinery across Shropshire, with the same rule holding for all three.

What we inherited

Rules we didn't write

Every one of these came out of that workshop. They're the reason the "what we won't do" list exists.

1

Explain it, or don't do it

If you can't tell the owner what changed and why, you've no business changing it.

2

Fix the cause

Anything else just moves the problem down the road for somebody else to find.

3

Turn work away

Saying no to a job you shouldn't take is cheaper than the one you shouldn't have taken.
