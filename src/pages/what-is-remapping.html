What is remapping?

No jargon, no sales pitch. If you've been quoted for one and want to understand what you're actually buying, this is the page.

Start here: your engine is a computer decision, thousands of times a second

Every engine built in the last twenty-odd years has a control unit, the ECU. It reads what the pedal is doing, how fast the engine is turning, how much air is coming in and how hot everything is, then decides how much fuel to inject, exactly when to ignite it, and how hard the turbo should blow.

Those decisions aren't calculated from scratch each time. They're looked up from tables of pre-set values written at the factory. A remap means rewriting those tables.

What separates a good file from a bad one

Anyone can buy the tool that loads a file. It takes twenty minutes and the car can't tell the difference. These are the questions worth asking whoever you use, including us.

"Is the file written for my car, or bought off a shelf?"

Same badge, very different story underneath. Mileage, service history, turbo condition and previous work all change what's safe to ask for.

"Will you log it before and after?"

Requested against actual boost, fuel trims, timing pulled, exhaust temperature. Without measurement, nobody knows what the file is really doing.

"Do you keep my original?"

If the answer is anything other than an immediate yes, walk away.

"What won't you do?"

A tuner who'll do anything you ask, including the illegal things, is telling you exactly how much care goes into the rest of it.

It depends entirely on the file, and on the judgement of whoever puts it on your vehicle. The manufacturer builds in margins: how hot things are allowed to get, how much pressure a cylinder can take, how lean the mixture is permitted to run. A good file moves fuel, air and timing together and leaves those margins alone. A bad file buys a bigger headline number by spending them, and it will still drive fine for a while, which is what makes it dangerous. A tuning box sits between the ECU and the engine and fakes the signals going past, so the ECU thinks one thing is happening while something else actually is. A remap changes the decisions at source, which is more precise and lets us respect the safety limits rather than work around them. Boxes have one advantage: they unplug.
