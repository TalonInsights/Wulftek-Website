Economy gains depend heavily on how and where you drive. We'd rather give you a realistic figure for your own mileage than a headline number you'll never actually see.

The mechanism

Nothing gets starved or softened

Economy work isn't about weakening the engine. It's about torque arriving earlier, so you use less throttle to hold the same speed and change up sooner.

Which is why we're upfront about the catch: the gain is only real if your driving lets it be. Short cold journeys, heavy traffic and a heavy right foot will cancel it out entirely. If your mileage doesn't justify the work, we'll say so.

Torque, earlier

Stronger pull from low revs so you short-shift naturally and sit in a higher gear at motorway speed.

Smoother part-throttle

Cruising stops needing constant small corrections, which is where a surprising amount of diesel goes.

Fewer downshifts

Hills and overtakes stop demanding a gear you didn't want.

Nothing removed

Emissions equipment stays exactly where the factory put it. Efficiency happens in the software.
