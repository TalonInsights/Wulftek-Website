Agricultural & plant

A tractor spends eight hours doing what a car does for eight seconds

Full load for seconds is an engine problem. Full load for hours is a cooling, exhaust temperature and transmission problem. It's a genuinely different job — which is why most tuning shops in this county quietly don't offer it.

The economics

Cheaper than the horsepower you'd have to buy

Machinery replacement costs have pushed most farms into keeping tractors far longer than they used to. A remap lets a machine you already own pull a wider implement or cover more acres in a day — at a fraction of what the next size up would cost you.

That's the whole argument. Everything else on this page is detail.

What changes on a machine

Torque that holds

Pulling power that stays with you through a long pass instead of tailing off as everything heats up. On heavy draft work that difference is measured in hours.

Steadier PTO work

Engine speed held more consistently as load varies, so driven implements run evenly rather than surging and dropping.

Fewer trips to the bowser

Better efficiency in the working band means more hours between fills and less of the day spent driving back to the yard.

Written to the driveline

The transmission is usually the real ceiling, not the engine. Adding torque a box can't hold is an expensive way to save money on fuel.

Emissions systems untouched

No DPF, EGR or AdBlue deletes on machinery either. It stays compliant, and just as importantly it stays sellable.

Reversible before a dealer visit

The original file is archived against the machine, so it goes back to standard whenever you need it to.

Practicalities

Nobody wants a machine off the yard in August

We work to your calendar — quieter weeks, early starts, and on-farm where the machine and the access allow it.

Will it affect the manufacturer warranty?
If the machine is in warranty, assume yes. You'll be told plainly before you commit, and the original file is archived so it can be returned to standard ahead of a dealer visit.
Does this stop it meeting emissions rules?
No. We don't remove or disable emissions equipment on machinery any more than on road vehicles. Everything stays fitted and working.
What about the transmission?
That's usually the real limit rather than the engine. We write to what the driveline is rated for, and if a machine can't take more torque safely we'll say so rather than take the booking.
Which machines do you cover?
Tractors, telehandlers and site plant across the main manufacturers — John Deere, New Holland, Massey Ferguson, Case IH, Fendt, JCB and Claas among them. If yours isn't on that list, ask: it usually still can be done. On-farm visits are arranged case by case depending on the machine and the access.
